Lecture 2: Reading Past Papers — 2005

Graduate Entrance Examination English study notes: Lecture 2: Reading Past Papers — 2005. Original examples, diagrams, and exercises are retained.

Key Points

Concept: The arguments and evidence of an argumentative essay

  • Divided into arguments and evidence, with the process serving the argument;
  • Unless the question is detailed, the argument can be skipped while reading;

Question Type: Reasoning and True/False Questions

  • Logo:
  • infer imply suggest learn from
  • Target Audience:
  • It can be a few sentences or an entire paragraph;
  • Method:
  • 1) Although it was not directly mentioned, rewrite the closest synonym
  • 2) Details follow the main theme: The key points that appear repeatedly are the key points, and the key points are the key points
  • 3) Reasoning is often set at the turning point
  • 4) Grammatical phenomena often include reasoning
  • 5) Relatively gentle tone: may,can,be likely to
  • Supplement:
  • Characteristics of the author's viewpoint -> Originality;

Question Type: Word Guessing Questions

  • Logo:
  • Double quotation marks;
  • Method:
  • 1) Finding contextual logical relationships;
  • Synonyms or antonyms;
  • 2) If the word to be guessed is a simple word, exclude its common meaning;
  • Supplement:
  • For each option in the vocabulary questions, if it is a word, carefully analyze its meaning, roots, and affixes;

Reading Summary

  • Opposite options in reading often contain the correct answer;
  • Comprehensive mastery of vocabulary from the three-year exam paper is required, as it is highly likely to appear in the following year;
  • Observe whether the that content is complete, then judge sentence types and modifiers based on their components;
  • Types of Postgraduate Exam Articles:
  • Social sciences (with economics being the most common) > Natural sciences > Humanities;
  • All options for the graduate entrance exam must be based on objective -> content appearing in the original text;
  • In the article, Now is often connected to the exam point, -> Now represents a change in time -> a turning point in events;
  • If the word in the option is comparative, carefully check whether it is comparative in the article;
  • see Keep up with the author's perspective;
  • Pay attention to whether the causal relationships in the options of the analysis example questions are consistent;
  • Double negative: No + No | No + Negative prefix;
  • Pay special attention to the comparative and superlative levels in the options
  • Comparative and superlative forms, if not found in the text, exclude;

Vocabulary Summary

Article: TEXT 1

  • rise: raise salary
  • Slack: slack, depression, -> negative emotions
  • be capable of: to be capable of accepting
  • as well: Similarly, -> means tautological repetition
  • rivals: competitors
  • Co-operative: Collaboration
  • above all: especially -> indicates important information or viewpoints by the author
  • inclined: inclination

Article: TEXT 2

  • associate -> as + soc (social) + ate (verb) -> association, connection
  • bought: to buy, to believe
  • parallel: parallel, similar
  • instead of: No... Rather
  • inflation: economic expansion
  • depression: economic decline
  • subscribe: subscribe, sign, agree
  • init: initialize -> initial: start, initial -> initiate: begin implementation, initiate -> initiative: measure, initiative, initiative;
  • apply: to apply, to adapt -> applicable: to adapt, to apply;

Article: TEXT 3

  • formulate: formulate: formulate, plan, form;
  • suspect: doubt -> certainty;

Article: TEXT 4

  • figure: n. number, object; v, inference;
  • nor: also -> means the same;
  • conspire: conspiracy
  • inspire: to motivate
  • expire: Debt due due date
  • respire: to breathe out
  • brought about change: to cause change
  • cult: passionate love;
  • spontaneity: spontaneous;
  • acknowledge: acknowledge, acknowledge -> in this chapter, following the author's viewpoint;
  • contempt: contempt, contempt -> negative attitude;
  • conservative: conservative
  • seek: to seek; to seek, to seek -> Past participle: sough
