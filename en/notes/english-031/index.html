Key Points

Concept: The logical relationships between sentences

  • 1. Concessions and Twists:
  • though；
  • although；
  • in spite of；
  • despite；
  • but；
  • yet；
  • however；
  • in face；
  • unfortunately
  • on the contrary；
  • now；
  • 2. Parallel relationships:
  • (1) Repetition
  • (2) Progression;
  • (3) Give examples;
  • 3. Causality:
  • Core: Clarify who is the cause and who is the effect;
  • Vocabulary:
  • because (of)； Follow the reason;
  • as: When followed by a sentence, it indicates cause and effect;
  • for: When followed by a sentence, it indicates cause and effect;
  • since：
  • so：
  • therefore：
  • consequently：
  • derive from: with reason;
  • originate from: with cause;
  • result from: following the reason;
  • owing to: to follow the reason;
  • due to: to follow the reason;
  • attribute to: to follow the reason;
  • contribute to: to follow the result;
  • lead to: follow the result;

Concept: The logical relationships between paragraphs

  • Summary:
  • 1. Explaining linearity—phenomena analysis—making evaluations
  • Analysis: (1) Advantages and disadvantages; (2) Comparison over time; (3) Causes;
  • 2. Raise the issue - original text analysis - solution/suggestion
  • Differences:
  • Whether the result is an evaluation, a solution, or a suggestion;
  • Topic Introduction to Patterns:
  • 1. Get straight to the point (the first paragraph gives directly);
  • 2. Analogy (starting with discussing other topics, but actually making analogies for other topics)
  • 3. Individual -> average
  • 4. Negative viewpoint (presenting an opposing viewpoint, then using transitional words to present a viewpoint)

Question Type: Central Idea Question

  • Method:
  • Stringing method;
  • Central syntax;
  • Form: Unique section
  • Form: Answer to the opening question
  • Lead word:
  • High-frequency words that repeatedly appear in the article are the center;

Reading Summary

  • Options include modern words, especially those referring to the problem;
  • Example Questions:
  • When the content in the example is A, and the example is followed by a transition word followed by viewpoint B, viewpoint B is the opposite of A, and A is the opposite of B;
  • In reading comprehension, when words ending with ly appear, you only need to focus on the degree of the meaning, not the specific meaning;

Vocabulary Summary

Article: TEXT 2

  • theatre: theater

  • give all this: In reading, it means "therefore," followed by a conclusion
