## 18.1 Basic Structure of Long and Complex Sentences
### 18.1.1 What is analysis of complex sentences?
**Analysis of Long and Complex Sentences**
- Principle:
	- $$\text{ Complex sentences (multiple sentences, many matters). }) \quad-(\text{ Cut off })->\quad \text{ Simple sentences (one sentence, one matter }) \quad-(\text{ Simplify })->\quad  \text{ The core of a simple sentence (the core content of a thing). })$$

### 18.1.2 How to break long and complex sentences
**Core: How to Segment the Sentence**
- Principle:
	- Where it was connected, where it was cut off
- Method:
	- 1. Find punctuation;
	- 2. Find the connective word;
	- 3. Analyze subject and predicate;

## 18.2 Break Long Sentences - Punctuation
**Three Types of Punctuation**
- 1. Comma;
- 2. colon;
- 3. Semicolon;
- Rarely appeared: 4. dash;

### 18.2.1 Comma
**Comma**
- Concept:
	- Usually, **commas do not appear alone**; they often appear together after adding a conjunction;
- Note:
	- 1. Exceptions `->` listed together, `and` may be replaced with a comma to appear alone;
	- 2. Prerequisite for using punctuation: the sentence must be connected, not the conjunction; commas in conjunctions must not indicate breaks;

**Examples**
- *The idea is intuitively compelling<u>, but</u> it doesn't explain how ideas actually spread.*
	- Comma + but: but is a conjunction indicating a transition;
	- First break: Break with a comma
		- 1. *The idea is intuitively compelling*
		- 2. *but it doesn't explain <u>how</u> ideas actually <u>spread</u>.*
			- Here are also finite verbs;
			- Can be broken again by the conjunction HOW;
	- Second segmentation:
		- 1. *The idea is intuitively compelling*
		- 2. *but it doesn't explain*
		- 3. *how ideas actually spread.*
		- Among 2, 3 is included as an object clause to explain **explain**;

### 18.2.2 Colon
**Colander**
- Meaning:
	- Chinese: Someone Says;
	- English: Further explanation;
- Concept:
	- 1. The colon generally does not require a conjunction and can be used as a conjunction on its own;
	- 2. Indicates an explanation of the previous content;

**Examples**
- *It's hard to imagine that many people are dumb enough to want children just because Reese and Angelina make it look so glamorous<u>:</u> most adults understand that a baby is not a haircut.*
	- There is a colon, so at least it can be divided into two sentences;
	- First segmentation:
		- 1. *It<u>'s</u> hard to imagine that many people <u>are</u> dumb enough to want children just because Reese and Angelina <u>make</u> it look so glamorous:*
		- 2. *most adults <u>understand</u> that a baby <u>is not</u> a haircut.*
	- The core of an event is the finite verb, so you can currently count the finite verbs and know how many things are being discussed;
		- Five finite verbs, so they can be broken into five segments;
	- Second break: according to the connective word
		- 1. *It's hard to imagine*
			- 'it' points to 'to imagine', with 'to imagine' as the subject;
		- 2. *that many people are dumb enough to want children just*
			- Object clause, indicating to imagine;
			- just: modify because;
		- 3. *because Reese and Angelina make it look so glamorous:*
			- Adverbial clause of reason;
		- 4. *most adults understand*
			- Content after the colon indicates further explanation
		- 5. *that a baby is not a haircut*

### 18.2.3 Semicolon
**Semicolon**
- Meaning:
	- Side by side;
- Concept:
	- Indicates parallelism;
	- Sentence One; Sentence Two; Sentence Three;
	- It can also represent a conjunction, so treat it as 'and';

**Examples**
- *Moreover, average overall margins are higher in wholesale than in retail<u>;</u> wholesale demand from the food service sector is growing quickly as more Europeans eat out more often<u>;</u> and changes in the competitive dynamics of this fragmented industry are at last making it feasible for wholesalers to consolidate.*
	- Break based on semicolons;
	- First segmentation:
		- 1. *Moreover, average overall margins are higher in wholesale than in retail*
			- moreover: indicates progressive expression, such as the first point, second point, third point...
		- 2. *wholesale demand from the food service sector is growing quickly <u>as</u> more Europeans eat out more often*
			- as: Introduces the reason: Europeans are eating out more often;
		- 3. *and **changes** in the competitive dynamics of this fragmented industry <u>are</u> at last <u>making</u> it feasible for wholesalers to consolidate*
			- The sentence is very long. You can find how many finite verbs there are;
			- The only finite verb `->` are... Making；
			- Reasons for long sentences `->` prepositional phrase connections
			- Analysis:
				- Short sentence: *changes are making it feasible to consolidate*
				- Preposition: *of this fragmented industry* in a fragmented market
				- Preposition: *in the competitive dynamics of this fragmented industry* In this fragmented competitive market;
				- Preposition: *it feasible for wholesalers to consolidate*
