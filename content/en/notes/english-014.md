## 11.2 Basic Concepts of Compound Sentences
**Coordinate status**
- Coordinate clauses have equal syntactic status;
- Compound sentences:
	- 1. A compound sentence contains two or more independent clauses;
	- 2. The clauses are joined by a coordinating conjunction, semicolon, or equivalent punctuation;

**List the basic concepts**
- Structure of compound sentences: multiple simple sentences + coordinating conjunctions;

**Summary**
1. Structure of compound sentences: multiple simple sentences + coordinating conjunctions (the four types of coordinating conjunctions are the focus);
2. Omission in compound sentences: identical elements can be omitted, while the remaining elements remain unchanged;

## 11.3 Coordinating Conjunctions
### 11.3.1 Coordinating Conjunctions Expressing Addition
**Addition and sequence**
- Illustration
	- ![Pasted image 20240312122808](/assets/notes/c96dd43a3a04-Pasted-image-20240312122808.png)

**Coordinates**
- And
	- ... And ...
	- both ... And
- Not only ... but also ...
	- `not only A but also B`
		- This pattern coordinates two parallel elements while adding emphasis;

**Examples**
- *the Renaissance was over <u>and</u> it was time for a new era—the Age of Reason.*

### 11.3.2 Coordinating Conjunctions Expressing Contrast
**Contrast**
- Basic concept:
	- 1. The two things before and after are opposites;
		- More goods are sold, but income decreases;
	- 2. The second clause presents an unexpected qualification or result;
- Note: In reading questions, important information often follows a contrast marker;

**Common words**
- `however` (a conjunctive adverb, normally separated by punctuation)
	- ... But ...
	- ... Yet ...

**Examples**
- *The environment is obviously important, <u>but</u> its role has remained obscure.*
- *We all understand that at some level, <u>yet</u> as medical consumers, we treat death as a problem to be solved.*

### 11.3.3 Coordinating Conjunctions Expressing Alternatives
**Alternatives**
- Choose between two or more possibilities;

**Coordinates**
- Or (choose one)
	- ... Or ...
	- either ... Or ...
- Neither ... nor ... (both alternatives are negated)
	- neither ... nor ...

**Examples**
- *<u>Either</u> Entergy never really intended to live by those commitments, <u>or</u> it simply didn't foresee what would happen next.*

### 11.3.4 Coordinating conjunctions indicating cause and effect
**Coordinating Conjunctions Expressing Cause and Effect**
- A reason may be introduced by `for`; a result may be introduced by `so`;

**Coordinates**
- Reason
	- ... For ...
- Result
	- ... So ...

**Examples**
- *Reporters tend to be part of a broadly defined social and cultural elite, <u>so</u> their work tends to reflect the conventional values of this elite.*
	- tend to be: often

## 11.4 Omission in Coordinated Structures
**Shared elements**
- Coordinate clauses or phrases may omit an element that is grammatically shared and recoverable from the first coordinate;
- Retain every element needed for parallel structure and clear meaning;

**Examples**
- *.... You don't“get”success but become it.*
	- Note: if two coordinated clauses share a subject and the second subject is omitted, the second finite verb remains part of its clause;
- *The program keeps track of your progress <u>and</u> provides detailed feedback on your performance and improvement.*
	- Here, `and` coordinates two verb phrases; the later `and` in `performance and improvement` coordinates two nouns;
	- Omitted:
		- The two coordinated verb phrases share the subject `The program`;
		- *<u>The program</u> keeps track of your progress...*
		- *<u>The program</u> provides detailed feedback on your performance and improvement.*
- *the peoples… ==died out== <u>or</u> ==became assimilated== <u>and</u> ==lost their native languages==.*
	- A coordinate structure may contain more than two coordinated verb phrases;
	- The three verb phrases share the subject *the peoples*;
- *He must <u>either</u> sell some of his property <u>or</u> seek extra funds in the form of loans.*
	- The modal auxiliary `must` is shared by both coordinated verb phrases;
	- Expanded second coordinate: `He must seek extra funds in the form of loans`;
- *the federal government must <u>support job training programs</u>, <u>raise the minimum wage</u>, and <u>fund more low-cost housing</u>.*
	- Before omitting:
	- the federal government must support job training programs；
	- the federal government must raise the minimum wage；
	- the federal government must fund more low-cost housing；
