## 17.1 What is an emphatic sentence?
**Introduction**
- Emphasis in spoken language: expressing emphasis through tone and phonetic stress;
- In writing, English can use a cleft sentence to **give one element special focus**;
- An `it`-cleft contains a clause resembling a relative clause, but its function is different: it divides information into a focused element and a background clause;

**Basic Sentence Patterns**
- Basic structure: `It is… that…`
- Example:
	- *I love you.* `->` *It is I who love you.* `->` Emphasizes that the person who loves you is **me**;
	- *I love you.* `->` *It is you that I love.* `->` Emphasize that **you** are the one I love;
- Focus: the element between `It is/was` and `that/who`;
- Background: the clause introduced by `that` or `who`;

**Note**: A cleft normally focuses a noun phrase or an adjunct phrase/clause. An adjective immediately after `It is` often signals an extraposed subject clause instead;

**Examples**
- *We hear the more honest argument only in recent years* `->` *<u>It is</u> only in recent years <u>that</u> we hear the more honest argument…*

## 17.2 Variations in emphatic sentences
**Variations in Emphatic Sentences**
- Structure: `It is/was… that/who…`
- Present-time focus: `It is...`
- Emphasize past events: `It was...`
- Generally, don't emphasize future events; if you do, use `It is...`
- `that` is widely used; `who` is also possible when the focused element refers to a person;

**Examples**
- *<u>It is</u> they, not America, <u>who</u> have become anti-intellectual.*
- *<u>It was</u> not until the 19 th century <u>that</u> the newspaper became the dominant pre-electronic medium...*
	- Past matters, using `It was`;
	- Emphasizing time: *not until the 19th century*
- *<u>It was</u> **only after I started to write a weekly column about the medical journals, and began to read scientific papers from beginning to end,** <u>that</u> I realised just how bad much of the medical literature was.*
	- Here, `It was` is used to indicate past events;
	- Highlights:
		- The focused element is the long time adjunct beginning with `only after`;

## 17.3 Emphatic Sentences and Postposed Subject Clauses
**Judgment**: Is it an emphatic sentence?
- Method 1: Reduction Method
	- Remove the `It is/was ... that/who ...` frame and reconstruct the remaining words. If they form the corresponding complete proposition, the sentence is a cleft;
	- Example: *<u>It is</u> this implicit or explicit reference to nature <u>that</u> fully justifies the use of the word garden...*
		- Removing the cleft frame yields: *This implicit or explicit reference to nature fully justifies the use of the word garden...*;
- Method 2: Elimination method
	- If an adjective after `It is` evaluates the following `that`-clause, the structure is usually extraposition rather than a cleft;
	- Example: *<u>It is</u> worrisome <u> that</u> society is medicalizing more and more behavioral problems...*
		- worrisome is an adjective `->` is postposed as a subject clause;
