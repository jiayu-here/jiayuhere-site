## 2.1 Changes in finite verbs
**Concept**: Finite verb inflection
- Three dimensions:
	- Tense
	- Modality
	- Voice
- Negation:
	- Affirmative vs. negative form
- Emphasis:
	- Emphatic `do/does/did`
- Note:
	- A finite verb phrase may contain several words, but the entire phrase functions as one predicate;

## 2.2 Tense of finite verbs
**This part is the most important, because every finite verb must have a tense**

### 2.2.1 What is Tense?
**The Concept of Tense**
- In this course, a tense form is analyzed as time reference + aspect;

**How to represent tense? **
- Tense and aspect are expressed through the form of the finite verb phrase;

**How many tenses are there in total? **
- Traditional teaching combines four time references with four aspects to present sixteen forms;

### 2.2.2 How to Choose Tense
**Analysis Method: Two Steps** `->` First time, then state

### 2.2.3 Eight Core Tenses
#### 2.2.3.1 Simple Tenses
- The simple aspect does not mark an event as progressive or perfect;

**Simple Past Tense**
- Finite verb = $did$
- Meaning: actions that happened in the past, things that happened entirely in the past;

**Simple Present Tense**
- Finite verb = $do/does$
- Meaning:
	- 1. Present states and general truths;
	- 2. Habits, repeated events, and regular occurrences;
- Which subjects use the third person singular?
	- `he`, `she`, or `it`;
	- A singular uncountable noun;
	- A singular countable noun;
- Example:
	- *The goal is new-search, not re-search.*
	- *Each year researchers publish millions of papers in more than 30000 journals*
		- Because **every year** new papers are published `->` at **a certain frequency, continuously** `->` Therefore, here is the simple present tense;

**Simple Future**
- Finite verb = $shall + do\text{ or } will + do \text{ or } am、is、are + going + to + do$
- Meaning:
	- 1. Refers to an event or state after the present;
	- 2. `will` and `be going to` can differ in context:
		- am, is, are going to do: pre-planned;
		- Will do: a last-minute decision (Will carries emotional willingness, indicating willingness);
	- 3. `shall` is uncommon in modern general usage and is rarely tested;
- Example:
	- *The problems <u>will be</u> different.*

**Future in the Past**
- Finite verb = $would+do \text{ or } was、were + going + to + do$
- Meaning:
	- 1. Describes a future event viewed from a past reference point;
	- 2. It often appears with a simple-past reporting or narrative verb;
- Example:
	- *They <u>gave</u> justices permanent positions so they <u>would be</u> free to upset those in power...*
		- `gave`: simple past;
		- `would be`: future in the past, viewed from the time when the positions were granted;

#### 2.2.3.2 Progressive tense
**Progressive Tense**
- Indicates that it is ongoing or actually underway;
- All are $be+doing$

**Past Continuous Tense**
- Meaning: An action was in progress at a past time;
- Finite verb = $was、were + doing$
- Example:
	- *He <u>was searching</u> for tiny engraved seals...*
		- The search was ongoing at the relevant past time;

**Present Continuous Tense**
- Meaning: An action or situation is in progress around the present;
- Finite verb = $am、is、are + doing$

**Future Tense**
- Meaning:
	- 1. Very few are tested;
	- 2. Events that are ongoing or will happen at some point in the future;
- Finite verb = $will+be+doing$

#### 2.2.3.3 Perfect Tense
**What the Perfect Aspect Expresses**
- The perfect relates an earlier situation to a later reference point;
- It may describe a completed event, a resulting state, or a situation continuing up to the reference time;


**Unified Formula for Perfect Tense**
- Finite verb = $have + done$

**Past Perfect Tense**
- Meaning: Standing at a point in the past, looking before `->` **Standing in the past looking before**;
	- Core: **Standing in the past, looking at past events**;
	- Present - (**Past - Past of the Past**) < - The content of this time period;
- Finite verb: $had + done$
- Example:
	- *The interviewers <u>had rated</u> applicants on a scale of one to five.*
		- The ratings occurred before another past reference point in the context;
	- *he expressed the opinion that in two or three respects his mind <u>had changed</u> during the preceding twenty.*
		- Ideas have already changed before expressing their views;
- Simple past tense VS past perfect tense
	- Similarities
		- All represent actions that occurred in the past;
	- Difference
		- Simple past locates an event in the past;
		- Past perfect locates an event before another past reference point;

**Present Perfect** (emphasis)
- Meaning: Viewed from the present, the present perfect links an earlier situation to the present;
- Finite verb = $have、has+done$
- Example:
	- *The ethical judgments of the Supreme Court justices <u>have become</u> an important issue recently.*
- Common situation: The present perfect often includes an adverb to emphasize `->` $already、just、ever、never、also、long$;
- Present perfect VS simple past tense
	- 1. Use the simple past with a finished past-time expression; use the present perfect when the time period or present relevance remains open;
		- Core in the present: emphasizes **it has an impact on the present**, present perfect: *He has left, so I cannot find him now*;
		- Core past: occurred in the past, simple past tense: *He left two hours ago*;
	- 2. For a situation that began in the past and continues now, the present perfect is normally required;
		- Doing this from the past to the present, and still ongoing: *We have learned English for 15 years.*

**Future perfect tense** (rarely appeared)
- Meaning: Before the future, up to the future;
	- For example: to complete this task by the end of this year;
- Finite verb = will have done
- Example:
	- *relaxation will be in front of smell-television, and digital age will have arrived*

#### 2.2.3.4 Perfect Progressive Aspect
**Perfect Progressive**
- Meaning: Emphasizes duration or ongoing activity leading up to a reference point;
- Finite verb = have been doing;

**Present Perfect Continuous Tense**
- Finite verb = have/has been doing.

### 2.2.4 A good example
- I love you
	- Simple present tense
	- I love you;
- I loved you
	- Simple past tense
	- I loved you;
- I will love you
	- I will love you
- I have loved you
	- Present perfect tense
	- The state began before now and remains relevant now;
- I had loved you
	- Past perfect tense
	- The loving occurred before another past reference point; the sentence alone does not state the present situation;
