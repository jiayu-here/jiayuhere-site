## 8.1 What is a prepositional phrase?
**Prepositional phrases are very important**
- Prepositional phrases are **very important and appear frequently in graduate English exams**;

### 8.1.1 Basic Concepts of Prepositions
**Preposition**: $prep.$
- A preposition normally takes a complement;
	- Its complement is commonly a noun phrase, pronoun, or `-ing` clause;
- Followed by three types of words, forming prepositional phrases:
	- 1. Noun;
	- 2. Pronoun in object form;
	- 3. Gerund-participial clause (`doing`);
- Note:
	- A preposition is not directly followed by a finite verb clause; a verb used as its complement normally takes an `-ing` form;
	- Prepositional phrases commonly function as modifiers or complements;

### 8.1.2 Key Points
1. Prepositional phrase = preposition + noun/pronoun/gerund (doing);
2. Pay attention to the different meanings of each preposition;
3. Prepositional phrases are an extension of the sentence; they are non-core components but are very common content supplements;

## 8.2 How to Use Prepositional Phrases
### 8.2.1 Prepositions indicating time
**Prepositions**: at/on/in
- These differ by the type and scale of the time expression;
- at: time point (specific time);
- on: days and dates;
- in: within the time (year, month, season, morning, evening)

**Preposition**: before/after
- Before/after... Time

**Preposition**: from/since
- Since... After time

**Preposition**: by/until
- Deadline (until)... Time

**Preposition**: for
- Lasting... Time

**Preposition**: during
- Within a stated period or event

**Examples**
- *<u>On a cold winter's day</u>, few culinary pleasures can match it.*
- *<u>In March 1997</u>, he lost \$72,186.*
	- `in` is used with a month and year;
- *<u>Since the days</u> of Aristotle, a search for universal principles has characterized the scientific enterprise.*
	- Since Aristotle;


### 8.2.2 Prepositions indicating place or range
**Preposition**: at/in/on
- at: a point or general location;
- in: inside an enclosed area or within a range;
	- Example: `in the room`;
- on: in contact with a surface;
	- Example: `on the table`;
- Example:
	- Person lying on the bed -> on/in -> on: lying on a hard bed; in: very soft bed, sunken inside;
- The meaning of a preposition depends on the purpose of your use and can be used flexibly;

**Preposition**: on/above/over
- On... above
- on: indicates the surface of an object that is in contact;
- above: Indicates various tops;
- over: indicates untouched above -> above the river surface;

**Preposition**: under/below/beneath
- In... Below

**Preposition**: in front of/behind
- Before... before
- After... Afterwards

**Preposition**: near/by
- At... Nearby

**Preposition**: beside/beside/next to
- At... Beside it

**Preposition**: between/among
- `between`: usually for distinct, individually considered people or things, not necessarily only two;
- `among`: within a group or mass;

**Preposition**: around
- In... Around

**Preposition**: into/out of/onto/off
- into：
	- Entering... Inside: dynamic changes from outside to inside;
	- in: to go, to be inside;
	- into: the process of going inside;
- out of: movement from inside to outside;
- onto: movement to a position on a surface;
- off: movement away from a surface;

**Preposition**: across/through/past
- `across`: from one side to the other across a surface or area;
- `through`: from one side to the other within an enclosed space;
- `past`: beyond a person or place;

**Preposition**: along
- Along with...

**Preposition**: from/to, towards
- From.../toward... going

**Examples**
- *But many <u>within the public sector</u> suffer <u>under the current system</u>, too.*
- Many people in this public sector suffer under the current system;
- *The link <u>between</u> dreams and emotions shows up <u>among</u> the Patients <u>in</u> Cartwright's clinic.*
- *Information flows <u>from</u> the media to the influentials and <u>from</u> them to everyone else.*
- Information flows from the media to influential people, and from those influential people to everyone;

### 8.2.3 Prepositions in Other Usages
**Preposition**: of
-... of... (indicates attribute)
- `A of B` commonly expresses possession, composition, relation, or part–whole structure;
- Two exceptions: A is a numeral OR a measure word
	- *two of the students* means two members of a particular group;
	- *the window in the classroom door* describes a window located in the door;

**Preposition**: about
- About...

**Preposition**: with/without
- Accompanied by with.../no...

**Preposition**: for
- Purpose: `for a specific purpose`;
- Intended recipient/beneficiary: `a gift for you`;
- Reason: `famous for its architecture`;


**Preposition**: by
- With... method, approach
	- by bus: to get there by taking the bus;
- Agent in a passive clause:
	- Passive voice + `by` identifies the agent;
- Difference or amount of change:
	- `increase by 10 percent`;

**Preposition**: as
- Role or function: `work as an engineer`;
- Comparison: `as tall as`;
- In some fixed constructions, concession: `as difficult as it is`;

**Examples**
- *They perpetuate the myth <u>of the lone genius</u>.*
- *It also sent a clear message <u>to</u> the outside world <u>about</u> his aspirations.*
- *Despite these factors, many social scientists seem reluctant to tackle such problems.*
	- Despite these issues, many sociologists are still reluctant to address them;

## 8.3 Past-Paper Practice
**Question**:*Few <u>of us</u> just walk straight <u>into the woods without a phone</u>.*
- of us: among us;
- into: to walk directly into the forest;

**Question**:*<u>For a time</u> literature showed no interest <u>in this public stage </u>.*
- For: can represent for..., can represent a period of time;
