## 1.1 The Core Structure of Simple Sentences
### 1.1.1 Noun + Verb
**The Core: Subject + Finite Verb**
- Subject: normally a noun phrase;
- Predicate: contains the finite verb phrase;
- A basic clause pairs a subject with a predicate;

**Finite verbs also change**

### 1.1.2 Classification of Predicates
**Action Verbs**
- Intransitive verb $vi.$
	- Meaning: does not take a direct object;
	- Pattern: subject + intransitive verb;
- Transitive verb $vt.$
	- Meaning: takes one or more objects;
	- Composition:
		- 1. Subject + predicate + object;
		- 2. Subject + predicate + double object;
			- Double-object order: **indirect object (usually a person) + direct object (thing)**
			- Example: send a person or organization something;
		- 3. Subject + predicate + object + complement;
			- A complement is a supplement to the object;

**Linking Verbs**
- Linking verb
	- Meaning: A linking verb connects the subject to a subject complement that describes or identifies it;
		- Example: *This **is** a dangerous game.*
	- Pattern: subject + linking verb + subject complement;
- Summary of linking verbs: How to **find linking verbs**
	- (1) forms of `be`: the most common linking verbs;
	- (2) `get`, `become`, `turn`, `grow`: express a change of state;
	- (3) `look`, `sound`, `smell`, `taste`, `feel`: sensory linking verbs;
	- (4) `seem`, `appear`, `keep`, `remain`;
- Example
	- *This courageous attitude in fact **becomes** a requirement for the performers of Beethoven's music.*

## 1.2 Past-Paper Practice
**Question**: *These views of arguments also <u>undermine</u> reason.*
- `undermine` is a transitive finite verb; `reason` is its object;

**Question**: *Even the word “habit” <u>carries</u> a negative implication.*

**Question**: *They suddenly <u>became</u> extinct.*
- Can be used as a linking verb: get, become, turn, go, grow

**Question**: *Our president <u>calls</u> himself the Decider.*
- `himself` is the object and `the Decider` is the object complement;
