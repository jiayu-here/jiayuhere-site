## 20.1 Subject-Predicate Model
### 20.1.1 Basic Concepts
**Subject–Finite Verb Analysis**
- Why it helps:
	- Long sentences do not always mark every clause boundary with punctuation, so identify each finite verb and its subject;
- Core:
	- First find each finite verb phrase, then determine which noun phrase is its subject;
- Common surface patterns:
	- 1. subject–finite verb + subject–finite verb;
	- 2. subject + embedded subject–finite verb + main finite verb;
	- 3. A subject clause followed by the main finite verb;
- Summary:
	- 1. A **noun + subject–finite verb sequence** often signals a relative clause with an omitted object relative pronoun; verify that the embedded clause contains a gap referring to the noun;
	- 2. Match each finite verb with its subject before deciding the clause boundaries;

**Two Models**: Subject-Predicate-Subject-Predicate
- Meaning:
	- Each subject–finite verb pair normally belongs to a different clause;
	- Use connectives and grammatical roles to determine which clause is main and which is subordinate;
- Method:
	- Do not decide main/subordinate status from order alone; check the connective and the role each clause performs;

**Two Models**: Subject-Subject-Predicate-Predicate
- Meaning:
	- The inner **subject–finite verb** pair forms an embedded clause;
	- The outer subject and later finite verb form the containing clause;

### 20.1.2 Analysis Example
**Examples**
- *The IASB <u>says</u> it <u>does not want</u> to act without overall planning...*
	- Two finite verbs, divided into two parts;
	- Structure: subject-predicate subject-predicate
	- Main clause: The IASB says
	- Clause: it does not want to act without overall planning. Object clause
- *Allen's contribution was to take an assumption we all share…*
	- Finite verbs: was, share
	- Structure: subject-predicate subject-predicate
	- Main clause: Allen's contribution was to take an assumption
	- Clause: we all share
	- Conclusion: `we all share` is a relative clause with omitted object relative pronoun `that`, modifying `assumption`;
- *When the court deals with social policy decisions, the law it shapes is inescapably political...*
	- Finite verbs: `deals`, `shapes`, `is`;
	- Punctuation; Conjunction When;
	- First segmentation:
		- When the court **deals** with social policy decisions； A finite verb, already broken and completed;
		- The law it shapes is inescapably political.; Two finite verbs, shapes and is, need to be further segmented;
	- Second segmentation: analyzing subject-predicate
		- the law it shapes is inescapably political…
		- Structure: subject-subject-predicate
		- Clause: it shapes -> modifies the law -> relative clause;
		- Main clause: the law... Is inescapably political
- *The way consumers now approach the process of making purchase decisions means that marketing's impact stems from a broad range of factors beyond conventional paid media.*
	- Finite verbs: approach, means, stems
	- Connective: that
	- First segmentation:
		- The way consumers now approach the process of making purchase decisions means -> Two finite verbs that need to be segmented;
		- that marketing's impact stems from a broad range of factors beyond conventional paid media.
	- Subject-predicate analysis: *The way consumers now <u> approach</u> the process of making-purchase decisions <u>means </u>*
		- Subject of approach: consumers
		- Subject of means: The way?!
	- Structure: subject-subject-predicate
		- Main clause: The way... Means
		- Clause: consumers now approach the process of making purchase decisions
		- Other: that marketing's impact stems from a broad range of factors beyond conventional paid media

## 20.2 Inspection Guide
**Strategy 1**: Use finite verbs to identify the clause skeleton before analyzing non-finite modifiers
- Content:
	- Begin with finite verbs; return to non-finite constructions after the clause skeleton is clear;
	- Focus on two aspects of finite verbs:
		- 1. Location;
		- 2. Quantity;
- Example:
	- *…an inability to consider the big picture was leading decision-makers to be biased by the daily samples of information they were working with.*
		- Finite verbs: was leading, were working -> Two things, separated into two parts;
		- Subject-predicate structure, with the latter part being the relative clause: they were working with

**Strategy 2**: When searching, look for clauses first; when reading, look at the main clause
- Example:
	- *The Dutch giant Elsevier, which claims to publish 25% of the scientific papers produced in the world, made profits of more than £900 m last year, while Uk universities alone spent more than ￡210 m in 2016 to enable researchers to access their own publicly funded research...*
		- Finite verbs: claims, made, spent
			- `produced` is a non-finite past participle modifying `papers`;
			- `funded` is non-finite here: `research being funded`;
		- Segmentation:
			- Main clause: The Dutch giant Elsevier... made profits of more than £900 m last year
			- Clause: which claims to publish 25% of the scientific papers produced in the world
			- Adverbial clause of contrast: while UK universities alone spent more than £210 million in 2016 to enable researchers to access their own publicly funded research


**Strategy 3**: Distinguish between main clauses and clauses to help solve and score points
- Opinion questions
	- Two Examination Methods:
		- The person who expresses the opinion
			- No mention of whose it is; all are the author's views;
			- Whose name it is, you can find it in the original text;
		- Examine the details of the viewpoint
- Example:
	- *The author believes that the devoted concertgoers___*
		- The first half is the main clause: The author believes
		- The latter is usually a clause: that the devoted concertgoers
		- This sentence testes:
			- The author's perspective...
			- Specific viewpoints within clauses...
	- Sentences corresponding to the above question:
		- *Devoted concertgoers who reply that recordings are no substitute for live performance are missing the point.*
		- Main clause: Devoted concertgoers... Are missing the point
		- Relative clause: who reply that recordings are no substitute for live performance
			- The clause contains the audience's viewpoint, while the main clause reflects the author's viewpoint;
