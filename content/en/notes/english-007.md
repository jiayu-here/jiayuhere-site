## 4.1 What is Voice
**There are two types**
- Active voice
	- The grammatical subject performs or controls the action;
	- *I eat apples*
- Passive voice
	- The grammatical subject receives or undergoes the action;
	- *Apples are eaten.*
- Choose the passive when the affected participant or result is the focus, or when the agent is unknown or unimportant;

### 4.1.1 Formation of the passive voice
**Form**: $\text{be}+\text{past participle}$
- The past participle carries the lexical meaning of the passive action;
- `be`：
	- 1. The form of `be` carries tense and aspect;
		- The lexical past participle normally remains unchanged while the auxiliary phrase changes;
	- 2. The finite form of `be` agrees with the subject where agreement is required;
- To name the agent: **be + past participle + by + agent**


**Method**: Three-step method for analyzing passive voice
- 1. Identify the passive lexical action;
- 2. Determine the required tense/aspect;
- 3. Make the finite auxiliary agree with the subject;

**Examples**:* Unfortunately, these critics have now been forgotten. *
- 1. Passive lexical action: `forgotten`;
- 2. Tense/aspect: present perfect, marked by `have`;
- 3. Plural subject: `critics`, so the auxiliary is `have`;
- Complete form: *Unfortunately, these critics have now been forgotten.*

**Note:** When an English passive construction is translated into Chinese, the natural translation may use either an active or a passive structure; an explicit passive marker is not always required;
- For example, Chinese may naturally express “The classroom has been cleaned” without an explicit passive marker;

### 4.1.2 Passive Voice and Tense Combination
**Each of the eight common tenses has a corresponding passive voice**
- Simple tenses
	- Past:
		- These critics were forgotten.
	- Present:
		- These critics are forgotten.
	- Future:
		- These critics will be forgotten.
	- Future in the past:
		- These critics would be forgotten.
- Progressive tense
	- Past progressive passive:
		- These critics were being forgotten.
	- Present progressive passive:
		- These critics are being forgotten.
- Perfect tense
	- Past perfect passive:
		- These critics had been forgotten.
	- Present perfect passive:
		- These critics have been forgotten.
- Summary: **The auxiliary phrase carries tense and aspect**
	- In an active finite verb phrase, tense/aspect appears on the lexical verb or its auxiliaries;
	- In a passive phrase, use the appropriate form of `be` followed by the past participle;

### 4.1.3 Combining Passive Voice and Modality
**Composition**
- Modal verb + be done = passive voice combined with modal;

**Examples**
- *In the internet age, at least in theory, this fraction <u>can be much reduced</u>.*
- *Those first few days should be spent looking for work*
	- The first few days should be spent searching for work;

## 4.2 Why Learn the Passive Voice
**Passive Voice Needs to Be Mastered**

**Why it matters:** The passive lets English foreground an affected entity, process, or result and is therefore common in formal and impersonal writing.

**Writing Question Type**: Passive voice is often used in writing;

**Translation Question Type**: Passive voice is also frequently used during translation;

### 4.2.1 Postgraduate Exam Reading: Synonym Rewriting
**Classic Question Formula**: Rewrite the original content according to the same meaning;
- Word exchange;
- Change synonyms;
- Active-passive interchangeability;

**Examples**
- Question: *According to Paragraph 2, the proposal might be regarded by some as __.*
- In the corresponding part of the original text read: *Some might see the proposal as a booby prize for the fact that.*

- Option: *can be taught to exchange things*
- Original: *teaching their monkeys to exchange tokens for food.*

## 4.3 Past-Paper Practice
**Question**:* "Benefits" <u> have been weighed</u> against "harmful' outcomes.*
- Been done: passive voice;
- have been weighed: Passive voice in the present perfect tense;
- The weighing occurred earlier and remains relevant at the present reference point;
- Passive voice translation questions:
	- 1. Identify the affected participant and the agent, if one is expressed;
	- 2. In Chinese translation, use either an active or passive structure according to natural word order;
	- 3. Do not force an explicit passive marker when the meaning is already clear;

**Question**:* This theory <u> was known</u> as diffusionism.*

**Question**:*People in earlier eras <u>were surrounded</u> by reminders of misery.*
- were surrounded: being surrounded... around;

**Question**:*Paid and owned media <u>are controlled</u> by marketers promoting their own products.*
- are controlled: currently under control;
