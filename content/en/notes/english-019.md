## 16.1 What is inversion?
### 16.1.1 Basic Concepts of Inversion
**What is inversion**
- Basic Concepts of Inversion:
	- This is to reverse the normal order of a sentence;
- Subject-predicate inversion:
	- In graduate English exams, you only need to master "**subject-verb inversion**," where the order of subject and predicate is reversed, meaning the finite verb is placed before the subject;
- Two types of inversion:
	- Full inversion: the entire finite verb phrase appears before the subject;
	- Partial inversion: an auxiliary appears before the subject while the lexical verb remains after it;

### 16.1.2 Full Inversion
**Examples**
- *Among the 10 worst performers <u>were</u> ==some of the most populous states==, including Texas, which was given the lowest grade (F) and has a disproportionate influence......*
	- most populous states: the most populous states;
	- `were` is the finite verb. The opening prepositional phrase is not the subject; the subject is `some of the most populous states`;
	- The full finite verb precedes the subject, creating full inversion. The structure:
		- 1. Connecting the previous and following lines, introducing the content of the subordinate clause;
		- 2. Gives prominence to the opening `Among ...` phrase;
- *Implicit within Tylor's definition <u>is</u> ==the concept==…*
	- Implicit: included within...;
	- `is` is the linking finite verb;
	- Here, `the concept` is the subject, and the finite verb `is` has been placed before it in an inverted structure;
- *Also unclear is <u>why Microsoft has gone it alone</u>.*
	- Emphasize unclear, so place 'Also unclear' first: 'Highlight unclear';

**Important sentence patterns**: `There be + noun `
- Meaning: existence
	- The `there be` construction expresses that something exists; it does not express possession.
- Concept:
	- Existential `there` is a dummy subject; the following noun phrase is the notional subject;
		- *There is a girl* states existence, whereas *A girl is there* normally identifies location;
	- There is a book on the table: objectively expressing an existence with a book on the table;

**Extended sentence pattern**: `There be + noun + prepositional phrase `
- Meaning of expression: expresses location or scope
- Example:
	- *<u>There is</u> a great deal <u>of</u> this kind <u>of</u> nonsense in the medical journals…*
		- Prepositional phrase;
	- *In some instances, <u>there is</u> absolutely no relationship <u>between</u> the content of the article and the citations.*
		- Use between to indicate the range;
	- *<u>There will</u> now <u>be</u> a seven-day wait for the jobseeker's allowance.*
		- now: indicates the current stage;
	- *There has been a kind of inflationary process at work…*
	- *…. But <u>there have been</u> controversial views about its economic. Political, social and cultural implications.*
	- *<u>There may be</u> more matches in the database..*
		- Combined modal: there may be
- Supplement: Be can combine tense and modal

### 16.1.3 Partial inversion
**Concept**
- Part before the subject, part at the back;
- How to select and perform partial inversion?
	- The word order resembles that of a yes–no question;
		- Do you like it?
		- *you like it.* `->` *Do you like it?*
		- *He likes eating.* `->` *Does he like eating?*
	- Yes–no questions use subject–auxiliary inversion;
- Method: **Front the auxiliary before the subject; if there is no auxiliary, add the appropriate form of `do`**;

**Scenario 1**: The negative adverb or phrase is at the beginning of a sentence
- Negative word: words that express a negative meaning;
- Example:
	- *... And perhaps <u>it has</u> never <u>served</u> so much to connect different peoples and nations before.*
		- never before: Never before
		- Inversion: *.... And perhaps ==never before== <u>has it served</u> so much to connect different peoples and nations as in the recent events in Europe.*

**Scenario 2**: An `only` phrase is fronted
- Example:
	- *<u>They will be</u> useful servants and not Frankenstein's out-of-control monster <u>only then</u>.*
		- Inversion: *<u>Only then</u> will they be useful servants and not Frankenstein's out-of-control monster.*

**Scenario 3**: The hypothetical conditional omits if
- Give an example
	- *But if Entergy had kept its word, that debate would be beside the point.*
