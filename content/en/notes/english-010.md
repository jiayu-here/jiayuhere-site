## 7.1 What Is a Determiner?
### 7.1.1 Basic Concepts
**What is a Determiner**
- Determiners occur at the beginning of a noun phrase and help specify reference or quantity;
- Common determiners and related noun modifiers:
	- Article: `a、an 、the`, etc.;
	- Numerals: one, two, percentage, fraction (e.g., two-thirds), first, second, hundred, thousand;
	- Possessive/genitive forms: `Mary's`, `the company's`;
	- Note: Nouns can also modify other nouns;

**Examples**
- *<u>The</u> roughly <u>20 million</u> inhabitants of these nations looked hopefully to the future.*
	- `The` marks definiteness; `roughly 20 million` specifies quantity;
- *<u>More than two dozen</u> companies sell DNA tests directly to the public, ranging in price from <u>a few hundred</u> dollars to more than \$2,500.*
	- Determiner:
		- `more than two dozen`: quantifies `companies`;
		- `a few hundred`: quantifies `dollars`;
- *Merrill Lynch got legal protection for <u>an asset allocation</u> strategy.*
	- The noun phrase `asset allocation` modifies `strategy`;
	- Paraphrase: a strategy for asset allocation;

## 7.2 Adjectives and Adverbs
### 7.2.1 What are adjectives and adverbs?
**Example Introduction**
- *The <u>angry</u> boy shouted <u>angrily</u>.*
- Angry boys shout angrily;
- The first is an adjective;
- The second is the adverb;

**Concept**
- Adjective
	- Adjectives modify nouns or function as subject/object complements;
- Adverb
	- Adverbs modify verbs, clauses, adjectives, and other adverbs;
- Both types add descriptive or limiting information;

### 7.2.2 How to Use Adjectives and Adverbs
**Method**
- 1. Their modifying functions;
- 2. Positive, comparative, and superlative degrees;

#### 7.2.2.1 Adjective
**Two Ways Adjectives Modify Nouns**
- Two common positions:
- Method 1: Attributive use `->` directly before, and occasionally after, the noun;
- Method 2: Predicative use `->` after a linking verb, describing the subject;
	- The linking verb connects the subject and its complement;

**Examples**
- *The idea seems <u>promising</u>, and Rosenberg is a perceptive observer.*
	- These are two sentences
	- `promising` is a subject complement describing `the idea` after the linking verb `seems`;
	- perceptive modifies observer, i.e., placed directly next to a noun;
- *Their thinking often had a <u>traditional superstitious</u> quality.*
	- Their ideas often have <u> traditional, superstitious </u> characteristics;
	- Two adjectives to modify;

### 7.2.2.2 Adverbs
**Adverbs modify verbs, sentences, adjectives, or other adverbs**

**(1) Adverbs modifying verbs**
- Give an example
	- *Most scientists and experts <u>sharply</u> dispute Hardy's views.*
	- Most experts and scientists strongly question Hardy's views;
	- *and the right mental workouts can <u>significantly</u> improve our basic cognitive functions.*
	- In multiword verb phrases, an adverb may occur between an auxiliary or modal and the lexical verb;

## 7.3 Past-Paper Practice
**Question**:*And where is the <u>nearest</u> water source?*
- Where is the nearest water source?

**Question**:* They'll get <u>less meaningful, less targeted</u> ads*
- `less` forms the comparative of both adjectives;
- The ads will be less meaningful and less precisely targeted;
