## 14.1 Basic Concepts
### 14.1.1 Calculation Theorem
#####**Theorem**: #Thecomputationalpropertiesofcontinuity
> <font color="#8db3e2"><font color="#c6d9f0"> description: </font></font>
> 1. The sum, difference, product, and quotient of continuous functions (with denominators not zero) are still continuous functions;
> 2. The**composite**of continuous functions is still a continuous function;
> 3.**Basic elementary functions**are continuous within their**domain**;
> 4.**Elementary functions**are continuous within their**definition intervals**;

**Explanation**
- Note:
	- The fundamental elementary function is continuous within its**domain**;
	- The elementary function is continuous within its**definition interval**;
- Defined interval: Everything contained within the domain is defined interval;
- domain is unique, and the defined interval is not unique;
	- Example: $1/x$, all values except for x=0 are domain; $(0,1)$ and $(1,+∞)$ are two independent defined intervals;
