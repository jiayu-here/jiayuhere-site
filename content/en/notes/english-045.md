This is a simple but enlightening drawing. Two students are standing in front of a notice board and discussing whether to attend a lecture. One argues that the lecture is unnecessary because it is unrelated to their major, while the other believes that learning something new will be worthwhile.

The scene presents two different attitudes toward learning. Diligence, confidence, and curiosity are important qualities for personal development. In reality, however, many students concentrate exclusively on their own fields and overlook the value of broadening their horizons.

Such a narrow approach can hinder long-term growth. Students should master the knowledge and skills required by their majors, but they should not limit all learning to a single discipline. Knowledge from other fields can stimulate new ideas and improve the ability to solve complex problems.

As a student preparing for the graduate entrance examination, I work hard to strengthen my professional foundation. In my spare time, I also attend lectures and read widely in the library. These activities expose me to new perspectives, deepen my enthusiasm for learning, and support more balanced development.
