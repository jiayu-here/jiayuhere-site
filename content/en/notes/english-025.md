## 22.0 Introduction
**Three Types of Special Structures**

- It is based on ordinary long and complex sentences with some variations;
- First restore the underlying structure, then apply the usual **segment + simplify** method;

## 22.1 What is a split structure?
**Split Structures**
- Overview:
	- Related parts of the core clause may be separated by inserted or postposed material;
- Core:
	- Find division and restore coherence;
- Method:
	- Identify the intervening material, reconnect the separated core elements, and then analyze the full sentence normally;
	- 1. Parenthetical split: temporarily set aside material marked by paired punctuation;
	- 2. Postposed-clause split: move the delayed clause mentally back beside the element it completes or modifies;

## 22.2 Parenthetical Split Structures
### 22.2.1 Split structures caused by appositive and parenthetical expressions
**Appositive and Parenthetical**
- Appositive: identifies or explains the preceding noun phrase;
- Parenthetical: Structurally, inserted into a sentence for explanation;
- However: When analyzing complex sentences, there is no need to distinguish between the two. Just recognize the inserted components;
- Features:
	- **Frequently paired punctuation**;

**Examples**
- *Bob Liodice<u>, the chief executive of the Association of National Advertisers, </u>says consumers will be worse off if the industry cannot collect information about their preferences.*
	- The appositive between commas identifies Bob Liodice and supplies background information;
	- After removing this part, the content is coherent:
		- Bob Liodice … says consumers will be worse off **if** the industry cannot collect information about their preferences.
	- Count the finite verbs:
		- `says`, `will be`, `cannot collect`;
	- Connective: if
	- Clause analysis:
		- Bob Liodice … says consumers will be worse off
		- Subject-predicate structure;
	- After segmentation:
		- Bob Liodice … says
		- consumers will be worse off
	- After analyzing it, retrieve the appositive and parenthetical words and continue reading;
- *These benefactors have succeeded in their chosen fields<u>, they say, </u>and they want to use their wealth to draw attention to those who have succeeded in science.*
	- Remove the parenthetical phrase they say;
	- These benefactors have succeeded in their chosen fields and they want to use their wealth to draw attention to those who have succeeded in science.
	- Analyzing finite verbs:
		- have succeeded
		- want
		- have succeeded
	- Can be split into three sections:
	- Conjunctions: and, who
		- These benefactors have succeeded in their chosen fields
		- and they want to use their wealth to draw attention to those
		- Who have succeeded in science.
- *Yet it is precisely these non-celebrity influentials who, according to the two-step flow theory, are supposed to drive social epidemics by influencing their friends and colleagues directly.*


### 22.2.2 Split Structures Caused by Postposed Clauses
**Concept**
- Three positions of adverbial clauses:
- Front, back, middle;
- In translation, an adverbial clause may be rendered before or after the main clause according to the natural information flow;

**Examples**

- *Peter Hardy, who sympathizes with the views of the energy sector, is resisting proposed changes to science standards for pre-teen pupils.*
	- Inserted part: who sympathizes with the views of the energy sector
	- Segmentation:
		- Peter Hardy…is resisting proposed changes to science standards for pre-teen pupils.
		- who sympathizes with the views of the energy sector
- *Yet these creatures are members of the biotic community and<u>, if its stability depends on its integrity, </u>they are entitled to continuance.*
	- Yet these creatures are members of the biotic community
	- and… they are entitled to continuance.
	- if its stability depends on its integrity
- *Branch points out that, even if a growing number of official guidelines and textbooks reflect scientific consensus on climate change, unofficial educational materials that convey more biased perspectives are being distributed to teachers.*
	- Branch points out
	- that … unofficial educational materials … are being distributed to teachers.
	- that convey more biased perspectives
	- even if a growing number of official guidelines and textbooks reflect scientific consensus on climate change.

## 22.3 Postponed Clause Split Structure

**Examples**
- *Contrary to the descriptions on record, no systematic evidence was found <u>that</u> levels of productivity were related to changes in lighting.*
	- Clause: 'levels of productivity were related to changes in lighting `->`' Explain the previous 'evidence' `->` clause moved after 'was found';
	- After moving back:
		- *Contrary to the descriptions on record, no systematic **evidence that levels of productivity were related to changes in lighting** was found.*
- *In 2005, IBM <u>noted</u> in a court filing <u>that it had been issued more Than 300 business method patents</u>...*
	- Where there is a gap between noted and clause in a court filing;
	- Therefore, you can move forward:
		- In 2005, IBM noted that it had been issued more than 300 business method patents in a court filing...

## 22.4 Past-Paper Practice
**Examples**
- *What is being called artificial general intelligence<u>, machines that would mimic the way humans think, </u>continues to evade scientists*
	- Split structures caused by parenthetical material;
	- Sentences are coherent:
		- What is being called artificial general intelligence... Continues to evade scientists.
		- evade: to escape, to make... confused (this question uses this method)
	- Parenthetical element:
		- machines that would mimic the way humans think - > Explanation of the term artificial general intelligence;
- *When one of these noneconomic categories is threatened and<u>, if we happen to love it, </u>we invent excuses to give it economic importance.*
	- noneconomic: having no economic value;
	- Inserted content: Conditional adverbial clause
	- When a species without economic value is threatened, we give it an excuse to make it economically valuable;
- *Though typically about two inches taller now than 140 years ago, today's people<u>-especially those born to families who have lived in the U.S.for many generations-</u>apparently reached their limit in the early 1960 s.*
	- especially those born to families who have lived in the U.S. for many generations -> Explain the term 'today's people' in a limited meaning;
- *Concerns were raised that witnesses might be encouraged to exaggerate their stories in court to ensure guilty verdicts.*
	- Clause: that witnesses might be encouraged to exaggerate their stories in court to ensure guilty verdicts `->` originally used to explain concerns;
	- But moved backward to the back of were raised;
	- Move forward:
		- Concerns **that witnesses might be encouraged to exaggerate Their stories in court to ensure guilty verdicts** were raised
- *This<u>, though it fulfills the laws and requirements ofFuturist poetry, </u>can hardly be classed as Literature.*
	- This -> The content following the subject -> is inserted
	- Main clause: this can hardly be classed as Literature
	- Clause: though it fulfills the laws and requirements of Futurist poetry -> Explain why it is difficult;
- *If connections can be bought, a basic premise of democratic society that all are equal in treatment by is undermined government*
